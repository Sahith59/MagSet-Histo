# Five-category BreakHis: independent research critique

November 15, 2026. This review inspected the corrected scope, the three five-category diagnosis reports, supplied Lang manuscript/poster and original EfficientNetV2B0 notebooks, current handoff, and primary-source papers/model documentation. It reviewed the new [first-comparison registration](../BREAKHIS_FIVE_CLASS_SPEC.md). It performed no classifier fit, encoder forward, model download, new image scoring, scheduler operation or source change. The recipe below is a **proposed follow-up**, not a registered or completed experiment and not evidence of 94–95% accuracy.

**Recommendation:** finish the registered frozen-Phikon direct-five versus dual-step comparison first. If a further supervised comparison is justified within the capstone deadline, choose **one plain EfficientNetV2B0 case-bag model with staged adaptation of its final two convolutional stages**, evaluated as matched direct-five and binary-plus-malignant-four pipelines. Keep masked mean pooling. Train case decisions directly; do not add the notebook attention blocks, another Phikon last-block retry, a larger-model sweep or a new split. This is a practical hypothesis with a bounded cost, not a claim that EfficientNet is the strongest available representation.

## The first registered comparison is defensible

The registration preserves original eight-label memberships before pooling, all ten 46/8/28 role assignments, four magnifications and all 15 subsets. Both arms share the accepted caches, fitting-only 46-case normalizer and case representation. The malignant expert uses only true-malignant fitting cases. The same four C values define four **complete-system** candidates per arm, with a shared C for the two hierarchy heads. Its 120 individual head fits and unequal head counts are disclosed. Selection uses end-to-end five-label mean-subset macro F1; hard routed labels are correctly distinguished from the composed probability vector's argmax. The fixed 0.5 router, convergence rejection and independent selection/reload replay are appropriate safeguards.

No scientific blocker was found in this document. Its improvement screen is explicitly descriptive, and a failure does not authorize a wider search. Before accepting outputs, verify the promised actual artifacts; reviewing a contract is not validation of an implementation or result. In particular, replay the disagreement fixture where hard routing and global five-probability argmax differ, and verify the malignant-head coefficient/class-order serialization separately from the binary one-row coefficient case. [Model review](FIVE_CLASS_MODEL_DIAGNOSIS.md), [error/metric review](FIVE_CLASS_ERROR_DIAGNOSIS.md).

This first study asks whether two declared decision pipelines behave differently on the **same frozen representation**. Head-specific balancing and the factorized loss also differ, so it cannot establish that hierarchy alone causes a gain. A later encoder change answers a different question and needs its own registration.

## What the supplied originals actually support

Lang et al. use ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 features, a benign/malignant XGBoost classifier and a malignant-four-class XGBoost classifier. Benign stops after the first stage. Their single-pass comparator is a direct five-class XGBoost model. They describe patient-separated 70/30 roles and report **image-level** results across magnifications; each input remains one image, rather than one case containing four image sets. Their Table 6 reports 85.32% versus 86.22% **binary accuracy**, and 55.83% versus 57.06% malignant-subtype recall. These are not 86.22% end-to-end five-class accuracies. The poster's single-pass subtype value is 55.89%, a small version discrepancy that should be retained if that table is reproduced. Neither source establishes a 95% result for the present endpoint. Methods/claims were checked in the supplied local originals; automated retrieval of the publisher DOI page timed out during this review. [Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.2582299), [existing original-evidence audit](CAPSTONE_ORIGINAL_RESULTS_REVIEW.md).

The two notebooks instead train flat **eight-class**, 40×-only EfficientNetV2B0 models. The code sets `base_model.trainable = True` despite a comment saying it freezes the extractor. One adds non-local attention; the other uses a ConvLSTM with a singleton sequence dimension, which is not a sequence of magnifications. Both then use global average pooling, Dense64/ReLU and Dense8/softmax. They resize complete fields to 224 square, use an image-random stratified 80/20 split and reuse the same validation images for checkpoint/learning-rate selection and reported evaluation. Fifty epochs, batch 64 and Adam are specified; the constructed early-stopping callback is not included in the callback list. These facts explain what ran, but do not demonstrate that those attention blocks caused the saved 93.73%/94.99% results or would transfer to case-separated validation. [Original-result review](CAPSTONE_ORIGINAL_RESULTS_REVIEW.md).

The corrected target matches Lang's **label hierarchy**, not its encoder, classifier, input unit or split procedure. Ordinary direct-five and dual-head neural training are valid comparisons; calling either a paper reproduction or a novel architecture would be unsupported.

## Why this follow-up, with its limitations

The local evidence favors restraint. The frozen/adapted case heads fit roughly 98% balanced accuracy but validate around 39–46% on the old eight-class endpoint. The supervised ResNet-50 reference also has a large fitting–validation gap. Another large trainable module can therefore increase memorization without solving generalization. The new specialist still has only 35 fitting cases: ductal 25, lobular 2, mucinous 5 and papillary 3. Benign pooling creates no independent malignant examples. [Model diagnosis](FIVE_CLASS_MODEL_DIAGNOSIS.md), [data diagnosis](FIVE_CLASS_DATA_DIAGNOSIS.md).

EfficientNetV2 was designed for parameter/training efficiency; its original paper does not establish superiority for this BreakHis protocol. The maintained timm card identifies `tf_efficientnetv2_b0.in1k` as author-trained TensorFlow ImageNet-1k weights ported to PyTorch, with 7.1M parameters for the original classification model and 1,280-channel pooled embeddings. It lists Apache-2.0 and shows no public access-agreement prompt. This supplies a plausible small model in the existing PyTorch workflow; no checkpoint was downloaded or its local availability verified here. This port would not be exact Keras-notebook reproduction. [Original EfficientNetV2 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/tan21a.html), [maintainer model card](https://huggingface.co/timm/tf_efficientnetv2_b0.in1k).

The proposed distinguishing change is **case-bag supervision**. The earlier supervised reference learns an image head from inherited case labels and then averages field probabilities. Here a sampled set of fields supplies one case loss after feature pooling. That removes the requirement for every individual field to independently show the case diagnosis. It does not guarantee that a small sampled bag contains useful tissue, and mean pooling can still dilute a rare diagnostic field. Those are limitations to report, not reasons to add a second pooling search. [Earlier supervised recipe](../BREAKHIS_SUPERVISED_SPEC.md), [saved-error diagnosis](FIVE_CLASS_ERROR_DIAGNOSIS.md).

Phikon-v2 already has a pathology-specific prior: its authors provide frozen CLS feature extraction and describe optional fine-tuning, including a LoRA example. That makes fine-tuning technically reasonable. However, the completed local last-block experiment supplied no consistent eight-class validation improvement. The corrected five-label task could behave differently, but re-running that recipe is not presently better supported than testing a small case-supervised CNN. Unfreezing more Phikon blocks or switching to LoRA would create another adaptation hypothesis with its own budget. [Official Phikon-v2 card](https://huggingface.co/owkin/phikon-v2), [completed adaptation](../BREAKHIS_ADAPTATION_RESULTS.md).

## One bounded follow-up recipe to register, if proceeding

The following choices are this reviewer's proposed engineering judgments, not settings proven optimal by a paper. Register them before new outcomes; do not treat this review as execution authorization or a completed specification.

| Component | Proposed fixed choice |
| --- | --- |
| Data and endpoint | Existing original-eight-derived ten roles; 46 fitting / 8 validation / 28 excluded; final order benign/DC/LC/MC/PC; all four magnifications and all 15 subsets |
| Encoder | One pinned `tf_efficientnetv2_b0.in1k` checkpoint with classifier removed; global average pooled field embeddings; record actual checkpoint, timm, torch and transform revisions before fitting |
| Representation | Mean field embeddings within each zoom; mean over available zooms; append four availability flags; Dropout(0.3); linear output head(s). No field attention, view gates, Dense64 bottleneck or additional normalization search |
| Arms | Direct-five softmax; or a binary router plus malignant-four softmax sharing that arm's field encoder. Separate model instances initialized from the identical pretrained checkpoint; no encoder pretrained on the other arm's fitted labels |
| Image processing | RGB; deterministic full-field bicubic resize to 224 square; use normalization from the pinned timm checkpoint configuration. Whole-field resize is an explicit override of any default crop, changes aspect ratio, and is not claimed to preserve physical resolution |
| Fitting augmentation | H/V flips, quarter-turn rotations, brightness/contrast/saturation factors in [0.9,1.1], fitting images only; deterministic augmentation keys shared between matched arms |
| Case bags | Each epoch visits each fitting case exactly eight times in a shuffled schedule. Each visit samples two fields per magnification without replacement where possible; use replacement only when needed. Same case/field draws for both arms |
| Loss and weights | Each bag supplies all 15 subset losses, averaged with weight 1/15. Direct uses final-five class-balanced CE; router uses binary class-balanced CE; expert uses malignant-four class-balanced CE on true-malignant fitting bags only. Dual objective is 0.5 router loss + 0.5 expert loss, with fixed global head normalizers, not batch-dependent loss scaling. No extra eight-class weighting, focal-loss or smoothing sweep |
| Optimization | Batch four case bags = 32 field images; AdamW, weight decay 1e-4, gradient norm cap 1. Epochs 1–5 train heads only at 1e-3. Epochs 6–20 additionally train the final two convolutional stages and final embedding convolution at 1e-5, heads at 1e-4; fixed cosine decay to 0.1 of these rates during that phase |
| Frozen state | Earlier encoder stages stay frozen. Keep all BN running buffers and BN affine parameters frozen, and verify hashes; map the trainable stage/module list against the pinned implementation. Disable encoder stochastic depth; head dropout remains active during fitting |
| Selection | Evaluate only epochs 5/10/15/20 using all validation fields and the deterministic processor; choose mean-15-subset end-to-end macro F1 with earliest-epoch ties. Four opportunities for each complete arm, same seed 812, no validation-driven LR/stopping/threshold change |
| Inference and records | Encode **all** fields belonging to the allowed case, then apply exactly the declared means. Dual uses malignant probability ≥0.5 and saves hard labels separately from composed five-probabilities/log probabilities, as in the first registration. No excluded-role forward |

The global loss normalization must be executable and tested. One explicit convention is direct/router weighted means over 46 cases and expert weighted mean over the 35 true-malignant cases, with every case represented eight times and 15 subsets equally weighted. A mini-batch estimator of the expert mean must correct for its 35/46 inclusion fraction; averaging only over however many malignant samples happen to appear in each batch changes the intended objective. The two objectives and learned encoder gradients still differ; this is a matched comparison of complete supervised pipelines, not an isolated topology ablation.

The staged, low-learning-rate choice follows established transfer-learning guidance: first train new heads, then adapt pretrained features cautiously. Keras specifically warns about large updates from random heads and maintaining BN inference behavior while fine-tuning. The proposed numerical settings and top-two-stage restriction are capstone choices, not documented BreakHis optima. If a Keras implementation were chosen instead of the proposed PyTorch port, its default EfficientNetV2 preprocessing expects float pixels in [0,255]; importing a second normalization convention would be a defect. [Official fine-tuning guide](https://keras.io/guides/transfer_learning/), [official EfficientNetV2 preprocessing documentation](https://keras.io/api/applications/efficientnet_v2/efficientnet_v2_models/).

## Cost and completion gate

Bound production at **20 model fits**: two arms × ten existing roles, each 20 epochs. The proposed draw budget is 46 × 8 × 8 = 2,944 field forwards per epoch, or 58,880 per run and 1,177,600 across production, before validation and final fitting diagnostics. Enumerating 15 masks reuses these embeddings; it must not rerun the image encoder 15 times. Only the last 15 epochs backpropagate into the permitted encoder stages.

Before any production launch, permit at most one fitting-only lifecycle smoke per arm, six epochs with at most two batches per epoch, covering the fifth-to-sixth-epoch unfreeze and save/reload. These two tiny technical fits must be disclosed separately. Require clean role allowlists, correct global weighting, frozen-stage/BN checks, permutation and missing-view invariance, finite losses, and reload parity. A smoke checks execution; it cannot forecast validation accuracy.

Use scheduler jobs, at most four concurrent GPUs, immutable source/configuration and resumable last/best checkpoints. A proposed 90-minute cap per production task bounds requests at 30 GPU-hours, or 7.5 hours in five ideal four-task waves; this is an allocation bound, **not a measured runtime or queue guarantee**. The fitting-only smoke must support a realistic projection, with at least a day remaining for full collection, independent arithmetic review and capstone packaging. If that cannot fit, preserve the completed first five-class comparison as the deliverable. Do not reduce roles/subsets or use a favorable partial matrix to meet the deadline.

## Stronger frozen encoders and access constraints

Stronger pathology features are a rational later direction. The original UNI study reports broad transfer/few-shot performance, while the Phikon-v2 benchmark finds no universal winner: larger encoders can lead on some biomarker tasks while a much smaller model wins others. Neither study predicts this five-category BreakHis result. A single new frozen cache plus the same registered heads would be less fitting work than fine-tuning, **if the chosen checkpoint and access were already available**. ## How to judge the follow-up

Keep the first five-class result and the supervised comparison separate. A simple improvement screen can reuse the first registration's +0.02 macro-F1 requirement on **both** arrangements, nondecreasing raw accuracy and positive recall for every final label; predeclare the exact reference and require the entire matrix. Report fitted parameter counts, checkpoint opportunities, all ten results, all 15 subsets, fitting–validation gaps, five-label macro F1/balanced accuracy/raw accuracy, routing sensitivity/specificity, malignant typing and probability quality. Do not select the architecture from one arrangement and report the other as confirmation.

The present case supports and reused validation make a very high score uncertain. Full-view accuracy, mean-subset accuracy, binary routing and oracle malignant typing are different endpoints. The 31-case validation union, unresolved clinical identity and development exposure remain unchanged. A score near 95%, if actually measured, would still be exploratory provider-case validation; it would not establish clinical utility, a new cohort, publication readiness or the causal superiority of dual-step classification. Failure should close this bounded comparison and feed the honest capstone report, without an automatic extension into more encoders, attention modules, thresholds or splits.
