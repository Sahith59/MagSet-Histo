## Training-math audit

No blocking loss, label-order or routing defect was found in the inspected paths. This source review does not replace the existing independent actual-output checks.

- The CNN uses `N/(5*n_class)` direct weights, `N/(2*n_group)` binary weights and `N/(4*n_subtype)` expert weights, with `N=46`. Each component has total full-epoch case-weight mass 46. Because the expert numerator is divided over all fitting cases, replacing its numerator with 35 would change its declared scale. Its benign contribution is zero and only true malignant fitting cases train the specialist.
- A configured denominator of `4*15` also for the final two-case minibatch is intentional. It avoids doubling those last cases' base gradient scale. Epoch logging recovers the weighted numerator divided by `46*15`. This does not assert equivalence to one full-batch optimizer step.
- The half-router / half-expert loss is a coherent registered choice. Its class-cost distribution differs from direct-five balancing; the comparison does not isolate hierarchy alone. Large rare-class weights and per-batch gradient clipping may interact with stochastic rare-case gradients, but the available artifacts do not establish that interaction as the cause of failure.
- The CNN adapts only the declared late modules; BatchNorm running buffers remain fixed. A nonzero adapting gradient and changed adaptive state are checked. Frozen BatchNorm is not evidence of a bug here.
- The final dual decision is explicitly benign when `r<0.5`, otherwise malignant subtype argmax, including malignant routing at a 0.5 tie. The composed vector `[1-r,r*q_DC,r*q_LC,r*q_MC,r*q_PC]` is used for probability scores. Its global argmax is correctly not substituted for hard routing.
- Stable log-sigmoid / log-softmax preserve log losses under probability underflow. The five-class metrics consume explicit hard decisions for accuracy/F1 and stable log probabilities for CE. The saved/reloaded class orders are checked.

The accepted frozen logistic specialist instead has effective training mass **35**, following balanced weights on its 35 malignant cases and `1/15` on the duplicated subset rows. This differs from the CNN's joint-loss expert mass for a stated reason. A new independent logistic specialist should match the frozen logistic definition; copying the CNN weights indiscriminately would change the effective regularization at the same `C`.

## What the present summaries can lose

With an affine head, `W*mean(z)+b = mean(W*z+b)`. This holds within fields and available views, including affine per-view normalization. The current linear head sees only first-moment image features and availability flags. Flags can change intercept terms; they cannot recover field heterogeneity discarded before classification or provide a distinct image-feature coefficient matrix for each view.

Probability pooling is different: generally `mean(softmax(W*z+b)) != softmax(W*mean(z)+b)`. It can preserve how much field-level evidence supports each label even when a small number of unusual fields moves the embedding mean. This is an architectural capability, not evidence that the discarded variation is diagnostically useful. A synthetic binary example with field logits `[-10,2,2]` gives sigmoid-of-mean 0.1192 and mean-sigmoid 0.5872, enough to change the hard route. It is a math fixture, not BreakHis performance.

The CNN changes both representation and optimization: ImageNet EfficientNet rather than frozen pathology Phikon, partial adaptation, stochastic two-field summaries with augmentation during fitting, and complete unaugmented field means during evaluation. For a frozen linear representation, sampled means can be unbiased estimates of complete means; applying a nonlinear loss after sampling does not make the sampled-bag training objective identical to the deterministic full-bag inference objective. This mismatch may increase variance. The completed experiment does not identify it, the encoder, image resizing, weak labels or optimization as a sole cause.

There is relevant negative evidence: the earlier **adapted eight-class** auxiliary field head's fixed mean-probability readout did not rescue its case result. Its 812/813 F1 was 0.3356/0.3033 versus 0.3840/0.3205 for its adapted case classifier. See [diagnosis review](BREAKHIS_DIAGNOSIS_REVIEW.md). The proposed recipe changes to frozen features, the corrected five-class target, explicit field weighting and newly optimized heads; those changes justify a bounded test, not an assumption that probability averaging will succeed.

## Exact candidate to register

**Inputs and identity.** Reuse the four accepted Phikon-v2 cache NPZ/JSON pairs, pinned encoder revision and trusted manifest/protocol hashes from `configs/breakhis-five-class-v1.json`. Reconstruct all ten original-eight-label role assignments before pooling labels. Do not resplit. Retain exact raw field paths, provider keys and label mappings. No new feature extraction, crops, stain fitting, augmentation or encoder adaptation is required.

**Normalization and heads.** For each role, reuse the same fitting-only per-view field-moment normalizer as the accepted case-mean model: equal mass per fitting case within each view. The specialist shares this normalizer, fit using all 46 fitting cases. Each field input is its standardized 1,024-vector plus a four-element one-hot magnification indicator. Fit a shared five-class multinomial logistic head for the direct control, and a binary logistic router plus a four-class multinomial malignant head for the requested two-step arm. There is no benign subtype head. Both arms have 5,145 stored head parameters, including biases, under the same explicit parameter counting convention. Using one shared field head avoids fitting separate experts from only two lobular cases per view.

**Field targets and weights.** Targets are inherited case labels, not new region annotations. Use `class_weight=None` and pass explicit `sample_weight`. For case `i`, view `m`, and one of its `F_im` fields, let `n_k` be the number of fitting cases in target class `k`, `N=46`, and `N_M=35`:

| Head | Eligible cases | Weight per field | Total fitting mass |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Direct five | All fitting cases | `N / (5*n_y*4*F_im)` | 46 |
| Binary | All fitting cases | `N / (2*n_binary*4*F_im)` | 46 |
| Malignant four | True malignant fitting cases only | `N_M / (4*n_subtype*4*F_im)` | 35 |

These weights give every target class equal total mass, every case within its target class equal mass, and every view within a case equal mass. They prevent field-rich cases/views from dominating. Do not additionally use scikit-learn's field-count-based `class_weight='balanced'`, multiply by another inverse-class factor, or add `1/15`: fields are not duplicated into 15 training subsets. At the case level, direct weights are `[.83636,.368,4.6,1.84,3.06667]`; binary weights `[2.09091,.65714]`; expert weights `[.35,4.375,1.75,2.91667]`. Each case weight is distributed over its four views and their fields.

**Optimizer and objective.** Use the accepted pinned NumPy/SciPy/scikit-learn environment, `LogisticRegression(penalty='l2',solver='lbfgs',class_weight=None,tol=1e-4,max_iter=2000,random_state=812)`. Treat convergence warnings as a failed fit, not successful completion. With these explicit weights, the objective is equivalent to weighted summed logistic loss plus `||W||^2/(2*C)`, with intercept unpenalized. Keep total sample-weight mass fixed as above so that the stated `C` values have the intended scale. Do not normalize every field head's weights to one while keeping the same `C`. The binary and expert heads are fitted independently with the same candidate `C`; there is no shared encoder gradient or extra joint-loss coefficient to tune.

**Inference finalized by the project lead.** For each case and each view, score every field. The direct arm averages five-class probabilities within each view, then averages the available views. For the dual arm, compose each field's joint five-class distribution `[1-r, r*q_DC, r*q_LC, r*q_MC, r*q_PC]`, then apply the same nested arithmetic probability averages. Derive the case malignant probability by summing its four pooled malignant entries, and derive the conditional subtype distribution by normalizing those entries. Route to malignant at probability at least0.5, including ties. The specialist is fitted on true malignant fitting cases. This finalized joint-pooling rule replaces the initial separate-router/separate-specialist-pooling proposal.

Use nested logsumexp for the within-field and across-view averages. Retain finite log probabilities and explicit hard-route labels. The source and independent collector reconstruct the same declared joint probability model.
